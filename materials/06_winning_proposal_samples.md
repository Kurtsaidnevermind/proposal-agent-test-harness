## The MAG voice, as these samples demonstrate it

Read the samples first, then check your draft against these traits:

- **Short declarative sentences.** Most run under 25 words. One idea each.
- **Active voice, named actor.** "Elena Vasquez holds the release," not "quality
  is ensured."
- **A number in nearly every paragraph.** Specific, sourced, checkable.
- **The customer's problem stated before our solution.**
- **No throat-clearing openers.** Never "in today's rapidly evolving landscape."
- **Banned words:** leverage, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class,
  best-in-class, synergy, holistic, innovative (as a self-description).
- **Concessions are stated plainly.** Where we are weaker, we say so and say
  what we did about it.

## Sample 1 - Technical Approach excerpt (FTA NTD recompete, won 2021)

FTA cannot defend a number it cannot regenerate. That is the problem this
approach solves.

Every figure we publish carries a query identifier. The identifier resolves to a
versioned query in our analysis repository, along with the input snapshot and the
analyst who ran it. When FTA asks where a number came from, the answer takes
minutes, not days.

We built this after watching a different program fail an audit. Three published
ridership figures could not be traced to source. The finding cost that program a
reporting cycle. We designed our pipeline so that failure mode is structurally
impossible: an analyst cannot publish a figure without a resolved query
identifier, because the publication step rejects it.

Our validation pass applies 340 rule-based checks and a statistical outlier
screen to every submission. In the first two reporting cycles under our current
contract, the validation error rate fell from 4.7 percent to 1.2 percent. We did
not achieve that by loosening the rules. We achieved it by returning malformed
submissions to the reporting agency within five business days, while the person
who prepared them still remembered what they did.

One caution. Rolling intake works only if reporting agencies respond. Four
agencies accounted for 60 percent of our late exceptions last year. We escalate
those four to the COR by name at day ten rather than absorbing the delay
silently.

## Sample 2 - Management Approach excerpt (Virginia DRPT, won 2022)

DRPT's risk on this contract is not analytical capability. It is turnover.

The prior contractor replaced its lead analyst twice in eighteen months. Each
replacement cost DRPT roughly a quarter of rework and relationship rebuilding.

Our voluntary turnover across analytical staff was 6.4 percent last year. The
industry benchmark is near 18 percent. We hold people by giving them the same
customer for years, not by paying above market.

Dana Whitfield manages this program. She has managed the FTA National Transit
Database support contract since 2021, directing 19 people. She is not a
figurehead PM assigned across six programs. DRPT gets 1.0 FTE of her attention.

Quality control sits outside the delivery chain. Elena Vasquez, our Quality
Manager, holds the release on any deliverable that fails second-pass review, and
she reports to the Chief Analytics Officer rather than to the Program Manager.
Schedule pressure cannot override a quality hold, because the person applying
schedule pressure does not write her review.

We will not pretend this is free. An independent review gate adds about a day to
each deliverable cycle. We build that day into the schedule rather than
discovering it at the deadline.
